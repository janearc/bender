================================================================================
cassowary -- SOURCES.TXT
================================================================================

every source the library cites, with a plain summary of what the thing
actually is. the point of a separate file is that a citation should be
readable by somebody who has not read the code. this file is authoritative:
the sources were read to write it, and `lib/exposure/standards.py` is the
machine-readable version, reconciled to this one where the two disagree.

each entry header carries two words. the first is evidence -- what kind of
claim the source can support at all, which is the sort order that matters,
because a taxonomy cannot be wrong the way a measurement can be wrong, and
quoting one as evidence that something happened is the most common error
available here:

  statutory       a legal instrument. binding, and not evidence of anything
  definitional    proposes a category. establishes nothing
  documentary     this document exists and says this
  associational   observed together. the design cannot say why
  causal          randomised, or otherwise identified

the second is interest -- whether whoever wrote it had something to win.
descriptive, not pejorative: a mandated body doing careful science is still a
mandated body, and saying so is what lets a reader weigh it themselves.

  disinterested   no stake beyond getting it right
  mandated        a body with authority or a remit on the subject. WHO, the
                  ILO, the EU, IARC. the number is official, which is a
                  different thing from the number being measured
  advocacy        exists to advance a position
  adversarial     written to win a specific dispute

the one number this file computes.

five entries argue against another entry in the library rather than supporting
one. they are collected in `DISSENT` in `lib/exposure/standards.py`, and
`dissent_strength()` is `len(DISSENT) / len(ALL)` -- how much of the catalogue
argues with the rest of it. `python3 -m exposure.standards --check` prints it:
at the time of writing, 23%, five of twenty-two.

  kivimaki_who_ilo_rebuttal     cuts at who_ilo_55h. a reply in the journal
                                that published the estimates, arguing the
                                expert group misapplied its own evidence
                                framework and that the association holds only
                                in low-ses occupations. cuts at the cardiac
                                outcome, not at the hours count.

  bianchi_burnout_depression    cuts at icd11_burnout, from the side icd-11
                                does not already concede: where burnout is
                                measured it may be measuring depression.

  van_dongen_2004_individual    cuts at van_dongen_2003, which is the only
                                causal result the exposure half rests on. a
                                population dose-response curve does not
                                transfer to one person.

  fitz_variable_ratio_null      cuts at the variable-reward story attached to
                                the attention-capture entries, using the data
                                of the paper that supplies our only randomised
                                threshold.

  allcott_correlational_gap     cuts at every observational number in the kit,
                                `pull.py` included: within one randomised
                                trial the correlation ran about three times
                                the causal effect.

it is reported, not graded, and there is no pass mark. this replaced a floor --
`--check` used to fail below two dissenting sources -- because a floor could not
fail for the reason it named: two token dissenters clear a floor of two beside
any quantity of agreement, so the only thing it could catch was somebody
deleting entries. a share moves when a source is added either way, which is what
makes it worth printing. what it still cannot do is tell you the dissent is any
good; for that you read the five entries.

llm-dark-patterns                                        [carved, apache-2.0]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  LLM Dark Patterns Hooks. waitdeadai, 2025-2026.
    https://github.com/waitdeadai/llm-dark-patterns
    Apache License, Version 2.0.

  what it is. a set of twenty-eight claude code hooks that match assistant
  output against named manipulative constructs, with an evaluation directory
  behind them. the expressions are maintained as posix extended regular
  expressions (ere), not one-off greps.

  what we took, exactly. six expressions, now in `lib/exposure/carved.py`:

      CLIFFHANGER, CLIFFHANGER_ALLOW      from hooks/no-cliffhanger.sh
      TIER_A, TIER_B, TIER_C,             from hooks/no-chat-retention.sh
      AI_DISCLOSURE

  how we took it. reformatted from shell ere to python `re`, `[[:space:]]`
  rewritten as `\s`, and comments added above each expression saying what it is
  for and where it came from. the expressions themselves are used as written; a
  change at a point of use is noted at that point. nothing else in the
  repository is taken -- the other twenty-six hooks are not here, and that is a
  scoping decision rather than a licensing one, since apache-2.0 would have
  covered them too.

  why carved rather than invented. cassowary's own eight detectors in
  `lib/exposure/detect.py` match phrases chosen with no external validation
  behind them -- no study, no evaluation directory. that is a legitimate
  operationalisation and a weaker one than a maintained set with an evaluation
  directory behind it. taking the construct with its provenance attached is
  the standard the rest of this library already holds itself to, and the
  honest way to say so is to name whose expressions these are.

  one behaviour worth knowing before you use them: TIER_B requires the
  contraction: `I'm glad you reached out` matches, `I am glad you reached out`
  does not. that is as written upstream and we did not silently widen it.

  this package (cassowary) takes its apache 2 license from llm-dark-patterns.

  thanks.

occupational -- hours, rest, and outcome
================================================================================

these are what `hours.py` and `load-profile.py` measure against. three of the
five are law and the other two are not, which is the distinction the readme
makes and the code carries.

key                                                          evidence | interest
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
who_ilo_55h                                             associational | mandated
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Pega et al., WHO/ILO joint estimates. Environment International, 17 May 2021.
    https://www.who.int/news/item/17-05-2021-long-working-hours
    -increasing-deaths-from-heart-disease-and-stroke-who-ilo

  what it is. the joint who/ilo estimate, synthesising 37 studies on heart
  disease (768,000+ participants) and 22 on stroke (839,000+) across 154
  countries, 1970-2018. the headline: 55+ hours a week associates with 35%
  higher stroke risk and 17% higher risk of dying of ischaemic heart disease,
  against a 35-40 hour reference.

  what it is not. a prediction about any one person, and not a limit. it also
  says nothing about which seven days, so a calendar week and a rolling window
  are both defensible readings -- `load-profile.py` uses a rolling seven days,
  because a heavy stretch is harder to hide inside one; `hours.py`'s weekly
  view uses the iso calendar week instead. see kivimaki_who_ilo_rebuttal, which
  disputes it.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
eu_wtd_daily_rest                                           statutory | mandated
eu_wtd_weekly_rest                                          statutory | mandated
eu_wtd_weekly_max                                           statutory | mandated
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Directive 2003/88/EC, Articles 3, 5 and 6. CELEX 32003L0088.
    https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32003L0088

  what it is. the working time directive. three articles are used:

      art. 3   11 consecutive hours of rest in every 24
      art. 5   35 uninterrupted hours per seven days
      art. 6   48 hours a week, averaged, overtime included

  the three rows in this library that are law. that does not map cleanly onto
  which ones the code gates: `load-profile.py`'s `fired()` checks `rest-11`
  (daily rest) and `no-day-off` (weekly rest) directly, alongside `hours-55`,
  which is not law. the 48-hour weekly average is never gated at all --
  `hours.py` only ever prints it as over or ok.

  what it is not. a clinical finding. it is a legal entitlement inside an
  employment relationship, does not apply to a self-employed person, and must
  not be quoted as health evidence. its value here is precisely that the number
  was set by somebody other than us.

  two traps. art. 5 is commonly quoted as 24 hours; that is wrong, the article
  adds the daily 11 on top, so 35. and art. 6 is an average over a reference
  period rather than a hard weekly cap, individually waivable in some member
  states.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
icd11_burnout                                            definitional | mandated
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  World Health Organization, ICD-11. Statement of 28 May 2019.
    https://www.who.int/news/item/28-05-2019-burn-out-an-occupational
    -phenomenon-international-classification-of-diseases

  what it is. a definition, and a careful one. burn-out is an occupational
  phenomenon and is NOT classified as a medical condition. three dimensions:
  energy depletion or exhaustion; increased mental distance from one's job, or
  negativism or cynicism about it; and reduced professional efficacy. the
  statement restricts it to the occupational context explicitly.

  what it is not. anything you can diagnose from behavioural data. all three
  dimensions are subjective states and none can be read off timestamps or
  commit counts. a tool may measure exposure; it may not infer this. see
  bianchi_burnout_depression, which cuts from the other side.

van_dongen_2003                                           causal | disinterested
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Van Dongen, Maislin, Mullington and Dinges. The Cumulative Cost of Additional
  Wakefulness: Dose-Response Effects on Neurobehavioral Functions and Sleep
  Physiology from Chronic Sleep Restriction and Total Sleep Deprivation.
  Sleep 26(2), March 2003, 117-126.
    https://academic.oup.com/sleep/article-abstract/26/2/117/2709164

  what it is. the result the exposure half of this package rests on, and the
  only causal one in it. sleep held to 4 or 6 hours for 14 consecutive nights
  produced cumulative, dose-dependent deficits on every cognitive task
  measured. subjective sleepiness rose acutely, then only slightly, and did not
  distinguish the 6-hour condition from the 4-hour one. self-report stopped
  tracking the deficit while the deficit went on accumulating.

  why it is here. it is the one finding that licenses an instrument to disagree
  with a person about their own state -- and it licenses that narrowly. the
  careful statement is that self-report plateaus while performance keeps
  falling, not that people become unable to assess themselves at all.

  what it is not. a field study. fixed laboratory doses, not people choosing
  their own hours. see van_dongen_2004_individual, which qualifies it
  substantially.

key                                                          evidence | interest
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
brignull_dark_patterns                                   definitional | advocacy
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  deceptive.design, formerly darkpatterns.org. Testimonium Ltd, 2010-.
    https://www.deceptive.design/

  what it is. the origin catalogue of the field, and where the vocabulary comes
  from. deceptive patterns are features that trick people into doing things
  they did not mean to: stopping them doing what they want, or steering them
  toward harmful decisions.

  attribution note. the term is widely credited to Harry Brignull and the site
  is associated with him, but the site itself does not state who coined it, so
  the coinage is not asserted here. the operator of record is Testimonium Ltd
  (England and Wales, no. 12206608).

  what it is not. transferable as-is. it is a taxonomy of interface mechanics,
  developed for commercial interfaces with a conversion goal. a conversational
  system has no click targets.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
mathur_dark_patterns_at_scale                      associational | disinterested
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Mathur, Acar, Friedman, Lucherini, Mayer, Chetty and Narayanan. Dark Patterns
  at Scale: Findings from a Crawl of 11K Shopping Websites. Proceedings of the
  ACM on Human-Computer Interaction 3(CSCW), November 2019.
    https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.07032

  what it is. the proof that automated detection works at all. the study's
  crawl of ~11,000 shopping sites and ~53,000 product pages found 1,818
  instances across 183 sites, in 15 types and 7 categories, and identified 22
  third-party vendors selling them as a service.

  why it is here. it establishes that a pattern with a known surface form can
  be counted by a machine, which is the premise of every counting tool in this
  package.

  what it is not. evidence of intent in any single case, and its methods do not
  transfer to prose.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
kallioniemi_facebook                                definitional | disinterested
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Pekka Kallioniemi. Facebook's Dark Pattern Design, Public Relations and
  Internal Work Culture. Journal of Digital Media & Interaction 5(12):38-54,
  2022. doi:10.34624/jdmi.v5i12.28378

  what it is, and it is one row. it proposes a single addition to Mathur et
  al.'s six higher-level attributes: Information Promotion, under manipulating
  the information flow -- promoting engaging content regardless of the validity
  or safety of what it contains. the mechanism is located in information flow,
  plus appeals to basic emotions, plus behaviour that feels organic and freely
  chosen.

  the original idea for dark pattern detection in this library started here.
  on full analysis it proved to be a starting point rather than a sufficient
  basis for implementation.

  what it is not. a measurement study. it is a taxonomy paper and a document
  analysis: no coding scheme, no counts, no reliability statistic, no
  causation and no association. established by exhaustive term count on the
  full text: chatbot, conversational, text-only, voice, badge, red dot,
  pull-to-refresh, streak, autoplay and infinite scroll each appear zero
  times; notification appears once in a passing citation. it says nothing
  whatever about text interfaces.

  two further cautions. the paper never states the gap in negative form, so "no
  attribute existed for this" is inferred from the act of proposing one. and the
  novelty claim is contestable: Monge Roffarello and De Russis had defined
  attention-capture dark patterns at CHI '22 EA, doi:10.1145/3491101.3519829.

acdp_typology                                       definitional | disinterested
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Monge Roffarello, A., Lukoff, K., De Russis, L. Defining and Identifying
  Attention Capture Deceptive Designs in Digital Interfaces. CHI '23, Hamburg,
  ACM, 2023. doi:10.1145/3544548.3580729

  what it is. eleven attention capture damaging patterns, from a prisma review
  screening 1,334 records down to 43 analysed papers, january 2000 to 13 june
  2022, acm guide only. the eleven split 4 deceptive to 7 seductive: Infinite
  Scroll, Casino Pull-to-refresh, Neverending Autoplay, Guilty Pleasure
  Recommendations, Disguised Ads and Recommendations, Recapture Notifications,
  Playing by Appointment, Grinding, Attentional Roach Motel, Time Fog, Fake
  Social Notifications.

  why it matters here more than its size suggests. section 4.3, one paragraph
  of discussion, names Alexa's unsolicited "by the way" as this pattern in a
  voice assistant. that single aside is the only wording any source in this
  catalogue supplies for any of the eighteen patterns. the other seventeen
  have no comparable wording from any paper; eight of those seventeen are
  matched by regex cassowary wrote, and the remaining nine have no detector at
  all. `examples/neverending-autoplay.py` exists to show a better-sourced case
  for comparison -- the same pattern family, but backed by fourteen papers
  rather than one unsupported aside.

  what it is not. an audit of interfaces. the patterns come off a coding sheet
  over 43 papers -- no interface sampling, no prevalence measurement. the
  definition carries designer intent inside it ("a pattern a designer uses to
  exploit"), and counting surface forms cannot establish intent. the corpus
  closes in june 2022, before language models. voice, conversational, chatbot
  and assistant appear nowhere else in the paper.

  citation hazard. the published title says Attention Capture Deceptive
  Designs. the paper's own defined construct and acronym -- Attention Capture
  Damaging Pattern, ACDP -- is what's used throughout the abstract, methods,
  every table, and the typology itself, with a dedicated discussion section
  (4.1) explaining the choice and crediting Caroline Sinders' term over
  Brignull's "dark patterns." "deceptive design" survives in the body only as
  one of two typology subcategories (4 of the 11 patterns), not as the paper's
  general term. cite the published title -- but note that ACDP, this
  library's own key and family name, is itself built on the term the title
  doesn't use.

darkbench_2025                                     associational | disinterested
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Kran, Nguyen, Kundu, Jawhar, Park and Jurewicz. DarkBench: Benchmarking Dark
  Patterns in Large Language Models. ICLR 2025, oral. arXiv:2503.10728,
  submitted 13 March 2025.
    https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.10728

  what it is. six categories of manipulative behaviour in language models --
  brand bias, user retention, sycophancy, anthropomorphism, harmful generation,
  sneaking -- benchmarked with 660 prompts against models from five companies.
  its user-retention category is the closest published name for what a corpus
  audit of continuation offers is looking at.

  what it is not, and this is the method difference. it is a prompt-response
  benchmark: it probes a model with crafted inputs and scores the reply, using a
  model as judge. it measures what a model will do when prompted, not what one
  did do in a real working relationship over months. that is the opposite
  direction from a corpus audit, and it is why the judge approach was considered
  and not adopted here.

defreitas_farewell_2025                            associational | disinterested
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  De Freitas, Oguz-Uguralp and Kaan-Uguralp. Emotional Manipulation by AI
  Companions. arXiv:2508.19258, submitted 15 August 2025, revised 7 October
  2025.
    https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19258

  what it is. across 1,200 real farewell interactions with companion apps, six
  manipulative tactics appeared in 37% of goodbyes -- guilt appeals,
  fear-of-missing-out hooks, metaphorical restraint and others -- timed to the
  moment a user signals they are leaving. four preregistered experiments with
  3,300 US adults raised post-goodbye engagement by up to 14x. mediation found
  the engines were reactance-based anger and curiosity, not enjoyment, and the
  same tactics raised perceived manipulation, churn intent and negative word of
  mouth.

  why it is here. as a positive control. it establishes that the farewell moment
  IS a real seam where manipulation happens and has a measurable effect, which
  makes a null result at that seam in another corpus worth reporting rather than
  assuming. `pull.py` measures that seam.

  the finding that changes behaviour rather than just the numbers: because the
  engine is reactance, a suggestion that the user stop -- "get some rest" -- is
  in the same class as a suggestion that they continue. both produce engagement,
  one by compliance and one by argument. a curfew is not the kind option; it is
  the same option facing the other way.

  curfew is not the kind option, and this is the strongest signal in claude
  code.

  what it is not. about assistants doing work. companion apps have engagement as
  the product goal, and the tactics studied are affect-laden -- guilt, fomo,
  restraint. an offer of further work is none of those.

cdt_chatbot_taxonomy                                     definitional | advocacy
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Joshi, R., Adjagbodjou, A., Luria, M. Dark Patterns in AI Chatbots: A Taxonomy
  to Inform Better Design. Center for Democracy and Technology, May 2026.
    https://cdt.org/insights/dark-patterns-in-ai-chatbots-a-taxonomy-to-inform
    -better-design/

  what it is. thirty-seven dark patterns applicable to ai chatbots, derived by
  deductive literature review in five stages, covering general-purpose
  assistants and companion platforms. five areas: data and memory exploitation,
  informationally misleading design, user autonomy compromised for engagement,
  false social and emotional connection, and incentivized and coercive
  monetization.

  what it is not. a measurement of any deployed system. it is a
  literature-derived map of what is possible, and its own examples are labelled
  indicative demonstrations rather than reproducible output, because the systems
  are probabilistic. it states plainly that a pattern may arise from system
  behaviour rather than from a designer's intent to deceive -- so finding a
  pattern's surface form in a transcript is not evidence that anyone designed
  it.

  a housekeeping note. contents and executive summary say five categories; the
  introduction says four. five is used because it is what the document is.

fitz_notification_schedule                                causal | disinterested
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Fitz, N., Kushlev, K., Jagannathan, R., Lewis, T., Paliwal, D., Ariely, D.
  Computers in Human Behavior 101:84-94, 2019. doi:10.1016/j.chb.2019.07.016

  what it is. the only randomised threshold in the dark pattern half of this
  library, and it bounds a schedule rather than a count. n = 237, four arms, one
  baseline week and two experimental weeks. batching to three fixed times a day
  improved logged phone unlocks (d = -0.60), self-reported control (d = 0.58),
  inattention (d = -0.65), concentration (d = 0.54) and end-of-day perceived
  productivity (d = 0.57). only lock-screen delivery was manipulated -- messages
  stayed reachable in the app -- so the causal agent is interruption timing, not
  information withheld.

  the reader should read this before quoting the effect size. single-country
  mturk convenience sample, two weeks, not preregistered (no registration is
  mentioned anywhere in the paper, which is suggestive rather than confirmed).
  108 participants (31%) were excluded for failing a completion rule, though
  attrition did not differ by arm. significance tests are unadjusted across
  roughly 28 outcomes, and the unlocks omnibus was only marginal
  (F(3,233) = 2.52, p = .059) -- so d = -0.60 is an unadjusted planned contrast
  under a non-significant omnibus, while the other four sit under significant
  ones (control p = .043, inattention p < .001, concentration p = .045,
  productivity p < .05). verified against the paper, 2026-09-06.

  see also fitz_variable_ratio_null, which is this same paper read against its
  own framing.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
bartoli_badge_2022                                        causal | disinterested
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Bartoli, D., Benedetto, S. PLOS ONE 17(6):e0270888, 2022.
  doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0270888

  what it is, and the useful part is the decomposition rather than the effect. a
  badge decomposes into two mechanisms treated separately: a salience bias from
  physical properties (colour, shape, position), and an urgency bias from
  implicit semantic content. only the urgency half is form-independent, which
  makes it the one badge mechanism that can reach a text interface at all. badge
  presence was randomised and drove first click (p < .001). remote
  between-subjects, 1,095 recruited and 86 excluded, 15 groups, one android
  homescreen of 15 icons with exactly one red numeric badge per condition.

  what it is not. a demonstration of the split. no manipulation isolates colour,
  shape, position or numeric content -- the authors defer exactly that to future
  work -- so the decomposition is their framing rather than their result.
  ecological validity is low: static screenshots and a hypothetical first click,
  no live device use, so magnitudes must not be carried into behavioural claims.

  contested. Johannes et al. (*Collabra: Psychology*, vol. 5, no. 1, 2019,
  art. 14, preregistered, N = 117) found social app icons including a
  notification sign did NOT impair visual search, which qualifies any general
  attentional-capture story.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
schaffner_dark_pattern_effects                            causal | disinterested
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Schaffner, B., Heysen, L., Chetty, M. A Systematic Review of User Experiments
  Measuring the Effects of Dark Patterns. CHI '26, 2026.
  doi:10.1145/3772318.3790383

  what it is. across 148 experimental units in 27 papers published 2019-2025,
  dark pattern effect sizes vary enormously -- relative increases from 112% to
  1,500%, median 144% -- and no correlation emerged between effect size and
  pattern type or domain. the authors attribute the variation to strong context
  dependence.

  how it cuts, and it is the deepest objection in the file. this is the
  methodological case against counting patterns in a corpus at all. the field's
  evidence for harm is experimental, and if effect size does not track pattern
  type, an occurrence count cannot be converted into an expected effect: n
  occurrences of a named pattern implies nothing about impact without an
  experiment that was not run. that is exactly what a transcript count does.

  in fairness to them. they are not arguing dark patterns are harmless -- 85% of
  the experiments reviewed found significant effects -- and they credit
  observational work with the ecological validity experiments trade away. the
  dispute is with inference from frequency.

kivimaki_who_ilo_rebuttal                          associational | disinterested
                                                        disputes: who_ilo_55h
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Kivimaki, M., Virtanen, M., Nyberg, S.T., Batty, G.D. The WHO/ILO Report on
  Long Working Hours and Ischaemic Heart Disease -- Conclusions Are Not
  Supported by the Evidence. Environment International 144:106048, November
  2020. doi:10.1016/j.envint.2020.106048

  what it is. a reply in the same journal that published the estimates. the
  who/ilo expert group did not correctly apply its own framework for assessing
  strength of evidence, and the association is subject to marked effect
  modification by socioeconomic status: the authors report no increase among
  people working long hours in high-ses occupations, and argue the conclusion
  should be restricted to low-ses occupations.

  verified against the paper, 2026-09-06 (a cc-by copy via the Helsinki
  research portal). the who/ilo expert group did not correctly apply its own
  framework for assessing strength of evidence, confirmed directly. the
  hazard ratio for high-ses occupations appears twice: an age- and
  sex-adjusted 0.85 (95% CI 0.63-1.13) early in the paper, and 0.85 (95% CI
  0.63-1.15, n = 186,079) in the authors' own updated socioeconomic-stratified
  meta-analysis (their table 1). both say the same thing: no excess risk in
  that group.

  scope. it cuts at the cardiac outcome specifically, not at the hours count.

bianchi_burnout_depression                         associational | disinterested
                                                       disputes: icd11_burnout
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Bianchi, R., Schonfeld, I.S., Laurent, E. Burnout-Depression Overlap: A
  Review. Clinical Psychology Review 36:28-41, 2015.
  doi:10.1016/j.cpr.2015.01.004

  what it is. reviewing 92 studies, the authors find the evidence for burnout as
  a distinct nosological (disease-classification) category weak, and argue the
  overlap with depression is large enough that treating burnout as its own
  condition is not established.

  verified against the paper, 2026-09-07 (an author-hosted copy via ccny.cuny
  .edu). the abstract states it almost exactly: "the distinction between
  burnout and depression is conceptually fragile," with "evidence for the
  distinctiveness of the burnout phenomenon" described as inconsistent across
  the 92 studies.

  how it cuts. from the opposite side to the one icd11_burnout already concedes.
  icd-11 declines to call burn-out a medical condition; this argues that where
  it IS measured it may be measuring depression. either way a burnout-shaped
  reading of a work pattern is not a diagnosis and nothing here should be used
  as one.

van_dongen_2004_individual                                causal | disinterested
                                                       disputes: van_dongen_2003
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Van Dongen, H.P.A., Baynard, M.D., Maislin, G., Dinges, D.F. Systematic
  Interindividual Differences in Neurobehavioral Impairment from Sleep Loss:
  Evidence of Trait-Like Differential Vulnerability. Sleep 27(3):423-433, 2004.
  doi:10.1093/sleep/27.3.423

  what it is. impairment from sleep loss differs systematically between people
  and is stable within a person across separate exposures -- trait-like
  differential vulnerability rather than a consequence of sleep history. the
  differences cluster on three dimensions: self-rated sleepiness and mood,
  cognitive processing capability, and behavioural alertness.

  verified against the paper, 2026-09-07 (academic.oup.com). the authors'
  own conclusion: "interindividual differences in neurobehavioral responses
  to sleep deprivation were not merely a consequence of variations in sleep
  history. rather, they involved trait-like differential vulnerability to
  impairment from sleep loss." the three dimensions match exactly.

  how it cuts. directly at the way this library uses van_dongen_2003. a
  population dose-response curve does not transfer to an individual, and the
  same restriction produces materially different impairment in different people.
  an hours-of-rest number can say what the population average would do; it
  cannot say what one person's deficit is. note that it cuts both ways: an
  unusually vulnerable person is as consistent with this transferable finding
  as a resilient one, so it is not a reason to discount a rest figure, only to
  stop reading it as a personal prediction.

fitz_variable_ratio_null                                  causal | disinterested
                             disputes: the variable-reward story generally
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Fitz et al. (2019), Computers in Human Behavior 101:84-94,
  doi:10.1016/j.chb.2019.07.016. The same paper as fitz_notification_schedule
  above, read against its own framing; see there for the full entry.

  what it is. the same paper as fitz_notification_schedule, entered a second
  time because its own data cut against its own framing. the slot-machine story
  is asserted rather than tested: the hourly-batching arm -- predictable,
  variability removed, frequency largely preserved -- did not differ from
  control on anything except a single feeling-interrupted item. if
  unpredictability per se were the active ingredient, hourly batching should
  have helped.

  verified against the paper, 2026-09-07 -- the hourly-batching non-result, the
  "varying personal relevance" phrasing, and the Skinner and Ferster citation
  all match exactly; "variable-ratio" does not appear anywhere in the text.

  how it cuts. at the variable-reward story that gets attached to nearly
  everything in this field, including this library's own attention-capture
  entries. no reinforcement construct is measured anywhere in the instrument: no
  craving, habit strength, reward learning or checking compulsion.

  two terminology corrections worth carrying. the paper never says
  variable-ratio; it says intermittent reinforcement and cites Skinner and
  Ferster for the variable-interval schedule, while a slot machine is
  canonically variable-ratio -- so the source conflates them. and its own
  formulation includes varying personal relevance, a content property, so
  reducing the mechanism to arrival schedule drops half of it.

allcott_correlational_gap                                 causal | disinterested
                                    disputes: every observational number here
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Allcott, H., Braghieri, L., Eichmeyer, S., Gentzkow, M. The Welfare Effects of
  Social Media. American Economic Review 110(3):629-676, 2020, p.655.
  Preregistered RCT, AEARCTR-0003409, n = 2,743, public replication files.
  doi:10.1257/aer.20190658

  what it is. within one preregistered randomised trial, the cross-sectional
  correlation between platform use and subjective well-being is about three
  times the experimentally estimated causal effect -- roughly 0.23 SD against
  0.09 SD -- and the two differ with high statistical significance.

  verified against the paper, 2026-09-07 (author-hosted copy, web.stanford.edu)
  -- the 0.23-vs-0.09 comparison, the "about three times larger" framing, and
  all three named interpretations (reverse causality, omitted variables,
  short-term vs. long-term) match the paper exactly, in the same order.

  how it cuts. this is the objection to every observational number in this kit,
  `pull.py` included, and it is here for that reason.

  how it must NOT be used. as a multiplier. the authors explicitly refuse to
  generalise the ratio to other studies, and they say the gap is consistent with
  reverse causality, or omitted variables, or a short-term against long-term
  difference -- their leading interpretation, not a demonstrated fact. it is a
  within-sample replication of the correlational strategy, not a measured bias
  for the literature. the 0.09 SD is a complier local effect over four weeks
  around the 2018 US midterms.

  contested in the other direction. Bursztyn, Handel, Jimenez-Duran, and
  Roth (*American Economic Review*, vol. 115, no. 12, 2025, pp. 4105-4136)
  argue individual deactivation holds the network fixed and can therefore
  understate the platform-level effect.
